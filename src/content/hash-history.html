Hash House Harriers: From Kuala Lumpur to Utah

A drinking club with a running problem

The Hash House Harriers—HHH, H3, or simply hashing—began in Kuala Lumpur in 1938. Inspired by the British game of hare and hounds, a group of friends laid a trail to work up a thirst and then gathered at the Selangor Club Annex, nicknamed the “Hash House” for its food.

The tradition returned after World War II and spread throughout Southeast Asia and then around the world. Kennels developed their own personalities, but the basic rhythm remained: a hare lays a deceptive trail, the pack works together to solve it, and everyone gathers afterward for circle and social traditions.

The goals recorded on a 1950 Kuala Lumpur Hash House Harriers registration card still capture that spirit:

  1. To promote physical fitness among our members.
  2. To get rid of weekend hangovers.
  3. To acquire a good thirst and to satisfy it in beer.
  4. To persuade the older members that they are not as old as they feel.

From the world hash to Utah shiggy

That worldwide tradition reached Utah in 1991, when Mark, Pam & Pwop carried it from Fort Worth H3 and laid Wasatch H3's first recorded trail on July 13. Lotsa Damn Shiggy H3 followed in 2011, SL,UT Discovery grew out of full-moon trails in 2013, and later kennels added old-school, social, and bike-hash traditions of their own.

As the Utah kennel family grew, the groups continued to organize and travel together. Surviving records show local hashers using the Whoreman name by February 2012 for a road trip to the Betty Ford Rehab Hash in Palm Springs. By December 2014, Wasatch H3, Lotsa Damn Shiggy H3, and SL,UT Discovery H3 were collectively described as the Whoreman kennels and were organizing events together.

Today, each kennel keeps its own schedule and traditions, while Whoreman brings the wider Utah community together for campouts, joint events, and Whoreman invasions when travel hashing.
